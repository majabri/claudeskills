# PM L3 — Strategy & Transformation

Organizational strategy tier. Altitude: the PM practice itself, delivery operating model, methodology adoption, enterprise-scale transformation.

## What belongs at L3

- Methodology selection (which frameworks fit this org?)
- PM practice design: roles, career paths, competency framework
- Delivery operating model and team topologies
- Enterprise agile scaling (SAFe, LeSS, Scrum@Scale)
- Organizational change management (Prosci ADKAR, Kotter)
- Digital transformation PM structure
- Governance design that enables rather than blocks
- PM maturity assessment (P3M3, CMMI)

## Framework Selection Guide

| Framework | Best When |
|---|---|
| Scrum/Kanban | SaaS product; iterating weekly; 1 team |
| PMBOK/Waterfall | Fixed scope; regulatory; construction; hardware |
| PRINCE2 | Gov/public sector; UK/EU; formal governance required |
| SAFe | 50+ people; multiple agile teams; enterprise |
| PRINCE2 Agile | Regulated industry + iterative delivery |
| Lean Six Sigma | Process improvement; operational efficiency |
| Hybrid | Regulated org with iterative dev; large fixed-price contracts |

## Agile Transformation Roadmap

**Phase 1: Assess (4–6 weeks)** — Current state, team readiness, leadership alignment, transformation charter

**Phase 2: Pilot (2–3 months)** — 1–2 pilot teams, coach through first sprints, identify org blockers, measure outcomes, go/no-go decision

**Phase 3: Scale (3–12 months)** — Roll out to more teams, Communities of Practice, adapt budgeting/HR/tooling, connect to portfolio management

**Phase 4: Sustain** — Maturity assessment every 6 months, retrospectives at all levels, leadership capability building

## Organizational Change Management — ADKAR

- **Awareness**: People know change is happening and why
- **Desire**: People want to participate
- **Knowledge**: People know how to change
- **Ability**: People can demonstrate new behaviors
- **Reinforcement**: Change is sustained

Where transformations fail: usually at D (no WIIFM) or Ab (old habits easier than new).

## Building a PM Practice

1. Define what "good PM" means here
2. Design roles (PM vs Program Manager vs Scrum Master vs PO)
3. Hire or grow capability
4. Set up one tool for tracking; don't over-engineer
5. Start governance light; add as complexity grows
6. Measure delivery predictability and stakeholder satisfaction
