# Scene Archetypes for Short-Form Video

Most successful short-form videos (60–90s) use one of a few proven structures. Use these as scaffolding when planning scenes — they're not rigid rules, but they reliably hold attention.

## The five-beat structure (default for message-driven video)

Best for: explainers, op-eds, social commentary, family/educational content, anything trying to convey a message.

| Beat | What happens | Approx. % of runtime |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | Grab attention. Open with a question, surprising statement, or warm scene-setting moment. | First 10% |
| 2. Setup | Establish characters, setting, the everyday situation. | Next 15% |
| 3. Problem / tension | Introduce the issue, conflict, or the thing the video is really about. | Next 30% |
| 4. Turn / insight | The pivotal moment — a question lands, an idea clicks, a character reflects. | Next 30% |
| 5. Resolution + message | Warm close, key takeaway, or call to action. | Last 15% |

**Example (90s family conversation video about responsible parenting):**
- Beat 1 (0:00–0:10): Family on couch, parents say "we want to talk about something."
- Beat 2 (0:10–0:25): Dad introduces the topic gently.
- Beat 3 (0:25–0:50): Mom and dad explain the problem and its cost.
- Beat 4 (0:50–1:15): Kid asks the pivotal question; parents respond.
- Beat 5 (1:15–1:30): Warm closing moment + voiceover takeaway.

## The hook-payoff structure (TikTok / Reels native)

Best for: viral-format social videos, single-creator pieces, anything under 60 seconds.

| Beat | What happens | Approx. timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | First 1–3 seconds must promise a payoff. "I tried X for 30 days." "Here's what nobody tells you about Y." | 0–3s |
| 2. Stakes | Why does this matter? Quick context. | 3–10s |
| 3. Build | The journey, the steps, the evidence. | 10–45s |
| 4. Payoff | The reveal, the result, the lesson. | 45–55s |
| 5. CTA | Follow / comment / try it yourself. | Last 5s |

**Tip:** The first 1.5 seconds determine whether anyone watches the rest. Open with motion, a question, or a striking image — never with a static title card or a slow zoom.

## The dialogue-driven structure (two-person conversation)

Best for: family scenes, interviews, debate-format content, emotional moments.

| Beat | Role |
|---|---|
| 1. Establishing two-shot | Set the scene, both characters visible. |
| 2. A speaks (close-up) | Introduces the topic or feeling. |
| 3. B reacts (close-up) | Listens, processes, asks a question. |
| 4. A elaborates | The substance of the message. |
| 5. B's question or pushback | The dramatic pivot. |
| 6. A responds with the heart of it | The emotional core. |
| 7. Beat together (two-shot or wider) | Resolution, warmth, silence. |

**Tip:** Cut between close-ups of each speaker. Hold the wide two-shot for emotional beats. Silence between lines is powerful — don't fill every second.

## The narrator-with-B-roll structure (explainer / essay)

Best for: educational content, opinion pieces, anything with one voiceover and supporting visuals.

| Layer | What it is |
|---|---|
| Voiceover | Continuous narration carrying the argument. |
| B-roll | Visuals illustrating each point — can be stock, AI-generated, archival. |
| Text overlays | Key phrases, statistics, quotes appearing on screen. |
| Music | Single track underneath, swells at emotional peaks. |

**Pacing:** Change the visual every 3–5 seconds. Static visuals lose attention quickly in short-form.

## The before-after structure (transformation / tutorial)

Best for: tutorials, recipe videos, fitness, makeover content, "how to fix X."

| Beat | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Before state | Show the problem, the mess, the starting point. |
| 2. Process | Quick cuts of the steps. Music carries it. |
| 3. After state | The transformation. Hold the reveal. |
| 4. Recap | One-line summary or key tip. |

## When to break structure

The structures above are starting points, not laws. Break them when:

- The user has a strong specific vision — follow it
- The content is genuinely unconventional (poetry, art piece, abstract message)
- The runtime is so short (< 15s) that structure compresses to "hook + one moment"
- The platform demands something specific (e.g., LinkedIn often rewards talking-head structure)

## Choosing a structure for a given brief

Quick decision tree:

- **Is there a message or insight to convey?** → Five-beat structure
- **Is this for TikTok/Reels and aimed at virality?** → Hook-payoff
- **Are two or more people talking?** → Dialogue-driven
- **Is it one voice over visuals?** → Narrator-with-B-roll
- **Is it showing a change from one state to another?** → Before-after

When in doubt, default to the **five-beat structure** for 60–90s content. It works for the widest range of topics.
